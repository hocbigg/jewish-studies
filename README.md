## Introduction

Jewish Studies is an interdisciplinary field encompassing more than three millennia of history, literature, legal traditions, and intellectual life. Rather than approaching Judaism through a confessional or theological lens, academic Jewish Studies applies historical-critical methods, literary analysis, and philology to explore how Jewish civilization developed, adapted, and sustained itself in ongoing dialogue with neighboring cultures.

This curriculum is built for independent, self-directed learners starting from absolute scratch. It assumes no prior background in Jewish history, religious literacy, or the Hebrew language. Whether your interest stems from ancient Near Eastern history, classical philosophy, religious studies, or modern geopolitics, this curriculum provides the shared foundation required to engage with primary sources and academic scholarship critically.

### Curriculum Structure

The five core subjects are organized to build contextual and conceptual literacy in a logical progression:

* **Foundational History and Text:** Start with **Survey of Jewish History** and **The Hebrew Bible (Tanakh) in Context**. Together, they establish the indispensable chronological framework and introduce the historical-critical reading of ancient Israel's foundational literature.
* **The Rabbinic Transition:** Move next to **Rabbinic Literature and the Talmud**. This subject covers the pivotal post-biblical era in late antiquity, when the destruction of the Temple spurred the creation of the Mishnah, Midrash, and Talmud—the bedrock of normative Jewish practice, legal reasoning, and communal life.
* **Philosophical Synthesis:** Take up **Classical and Modern Jewish Thought** once you have a firm grasp of biblical and rabbinic concepts. This track traces how Jewish thinkers systematically engaged with external intellectual currents, from medieval Greco-Arabic philosophy through modern Enlightenment philosophy, Zionism, and post-Holocaust thought.
* **Language Training:** **Introduction to Biblical Hebrew** teaches original-language reading skills. Because language acquisition requires consistent, habitual drill rather than rapid reading, it is best studied in parallel alongside the historical and textual tracks rather than treated as a separate obstacle to clear first.

### Scope

This page is deliberately conservative in scope: it covers only the core knowledge every student in the discipline needs before branching out. Once you have worked through these foundational subjects, you can explore specialized subfields in [Advanced Topics](advanced_topics.md).

## Curriculum

### Survey of Jewish History

A comprehensive chronological survey of Jewish historical experience from ancient Near Eastern origins and late antiquity through the medieval diaspora, the Haskalah, the Holocaust, and the establishment of the State of Israel.

[Crash Course in Jewish History by Dr. Henry Abramson (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLdiAQwLyKa8LuYvrd4rn1y7ImOyhRbeTn) - A free, four-part university lecture series spanning ancient, medieval, modern, and contemporary Jewish history, serving as an accessible visual introduction.

[A Short History of the Jewish People: From Legendary Times to Modern Statehood by Raymond P. Scheindlin (Google Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780195139419) - The standard concise academic textbook for undergraduates, best read alongside the Abramson lectures for a structured narrative overview.

[A History of the Jewish People by H. H. Ben-Sasson (Google Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780674397316) - A comprehensive, highly detailed alternative to Scheindlin that provides exhaustive scholarly depth on political, cultural, and communal institutions across millennia.

### The Hebrew Bible (Tanakh) in Context

An academic introduction to the literary genres, historical contexts, and theological compositions of the Torah, Prophets (Nevi'im), and Writings (Ketuvim) using the historical-critical method.

[Introduction to the Old Testament (Hebrew Bible) by Christine Hayes (Yale Open Courses)](https://oyc.yale.edu/religious-studies/rlst-145) - A foundational, free 24-lecture university course covering the ancient Near Eastern context, source criticism, and literary development of biblical texts.

[The Jewish Study Bible: Second Edition by Adele Berlin and Marc Zvi Brettler (Google Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780199978465) - The premier academic study Bible featuring the Jewish Publication Society translation, scholarly notes, and contextual essays designed to be read sequentially with the Hayes lectures.

### Rabbinic Literature and the Talmud

An examination of the emergence of Rabbinic Judaism, focusing on the historical origins, hermeneutical methods, and literary structures of the Mishnah, Midrash, and Babylonian Talmud.

[The Talmud: A Methodological Introduction (Northwestern University / Coursera)](https://www.coursera.org/learn/the-talmud) - A free-to-audit 8-module university course that walks beginners through rabbinic case law, legal dialectics, and the composition of a Talmudic sugya.

[The Essential Talmud by Adin Steinsaltz (Google Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780465082735) - A clear conceptual overview explaining the history, structure, and internal worldview of the Talmud, ideal for reading before or alongside the Coursera lectures.

[Back to the Sources: Reading the Classic Jewish Texts by Barry W. Holtz (Google Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780671605964) - A guided reader that complements Steinsaltz by demonstrating, line by line, how to decode and interpret primary selections of Mishnah, Talmud, and Midrash.

### Classical and Modern Jewish Thought

An exploration of how Jewish thinkers have articulated identity, theological dilemmas, and ethics when encountering broader philosophical movements, from medieval Islamic Aristotelianism to the Enlightenment, Zionism, and post-Holocaust thought.

[A History of Mediaeval Jewish Philosophy by Isaac Husik (Internet Archive)](https://archive.org/details/historyofmediaev00husi) - A classic, comprehensive public-domain survey detailing medieval rationalism from Saadia Gaon through Maimonides and Joseph Albo.

[The Cambridge Companion to Modern Jewish Philosophy by Michael L. Morgan and Peter Eli Gordon (Google Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780521012553) - A chronological continuation of Husik, providing scholarly chapters on modern intellectual figures including Spinoza, Mendelssohn, Buber, Rosenzweig, and post-Holocaust theologians.

### Introduction to Biblical Hebrew

Foundational training in the orthography, phonology, vocabulary, and basic grammatical syntax necessary for reading prose narratives in the Hebrew Bible.

[Aleph with Beth: Biblical Hebrew Lessons 1–50 in Order (YouTube)](https://www.youtube.com/playlist?list=PLq1vmb-z7PpQt2PDNUr7XOzBjWAOWf0Rt) - A completely free video immersion series designed for absolute beginners to develop intuitive vocabulary acquisition and listening comprehension.

[The First Hebrew Primer: The Adult Beginner's Path to Biblical Hebrew by Ethelyn Simon, Linda Motzkin, and Irene Resnikoff (Google Books)](https://books.google.com/books?isbn=9780939144150) - A deductive textbook and workbook tailored for independent adult study that provides the formal grammatical explanations, paradigm charts, and reading exercises meant to reinforce the video lessons.
